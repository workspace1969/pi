01Price Anchoring

The Two Numbers

The Angle
Put the repair bill and the service call fee in the same frame and let the gap do the arguing. The second label now names the product, so the $100 is understood as what a Simplicity home warranty costs you at the moment of a claim.
Why It Works
Home warranty is a category people do not shop for, they get shocked into. The buyer has no reference price for coverage but a vivid one for a failed air conditioner. Anchoring high and resolving low turns an abstract product into obvious arithmetic in under two seconds. The $6,950 figure is already used in the agent email, so the number is defensible and consistent across channels.

05Category Attack

Claims, Not Disclaimers

The Angle
The line stays, but the support underneath had to earn it. Saying we pay covered claims is what every competitor says, so the proof is now the thing that is actually unusual: exclusions printed next to inclusions, on the page, before money changes hands.
Why It Works
Home warranty carries a real reputation problem, and the buyer arrives assuming the contract is written to avoid paying. A brand that says the quiet part out loud reframes the click from get a price to see whether these people are different. The headline is only credible if the line under it is a claim a competitor could not copy, and every contract having exclusions is not that claim. Showing them up front is, and it is something the funnel genuinely does.

06Social Proof At Scale

Four Thousand Reviews

The Angle
Rebuilt around the Google reviews rather than the company history. The volume of reviews is the headline, the years of claims paying drops to a supporting line, and the product is named in between.
Why It Works
Credibility is load bearing in this category rather than decorative, and it is what affiliate traffic is shortest on, because the visitor has no idea who Simplicity is at the moment of the click.
